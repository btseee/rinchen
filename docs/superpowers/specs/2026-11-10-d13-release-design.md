# D13 Final evaluation and release tooling: design

Status: binding for the D13 plan (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-10-d13-release.md`), written before the build; the last task of the plan amends it to the finished code (section 7, amendments, as D12's). The runbook is `docs/runbooks/13_release.md`. Master plan: `docs/research/2026-10-29-rinchen-dev-agent-prompt.md`, milestone D13 (and D14, D15 for what D13 must leave room for). Evidence for every fact about sacrebleu, the bootstrap, `datasets`, `huggingface_hub`, the engine, CTranslate2 baselines, GitHub Actions and the toy: `.superpowers/sdd/2026-11-10-d13-release/spike-findings.md` (a spike on this machine, CPU, offline, toy data; the finding numbers F1 to F18 are cited below). Everything GPU-side, every real dataset, baseline and neural metric, and every network step is UNMEASURED and labelled so.

## 1. Goal and non-goals

Goal: say, with numbers that can be recomputed from files and with intervals that mean something, how a model of ours translates a benchmark, how it compares with another system, and ship it with a card that says only what was measured. D13 builds the benchmark suite file (FLORES+, NTREX, the Mongolia-sourced human sets of D2) and the builders that make it from LOCAL files, the one fetch step that touches the network (a thin use of D1's `acquire.hf.fetch`, mocked in every test); the systems (a Rinchen export through `Engine`, an NLLB or MADLAD directory or a vLLM server through D6's generators, any system through a hypotheses file) and the runner; BLEU, chrF and chrF++ with their sacrebleu signatures from per-segment sufficient statistics; a paired bootstrap of 1,000 resamples with a fixed seed (our own, exact, independent of numpy's generator); the result `result.json` per (suite, system, direction) and the aggregate `report.json` and `report.md`, which embed D12's robustness block with its coverage; the seam through which COMET and MetricX scores enter; the model card (`export/model_card.py`), the release staging directory and the Hub upload plan (`export/hub.py`) with a dry run that lists exactly what would leave the machine; `release.yml`; and the commands `rinchen eval suite|run|report` and `rinchen release stage|hub`. STOP gate (master plan: "tiny report.md + model card generated; dry-run hub upload (mock)"): through the real `rinchen` binary on the CPU with the GPU hidden, the toy export of D12 (and the D11 teacher as a second system) translates a FLORES-like fixture suite built from the toy, the results, the report (JSON and Markdown) and the model card are generated, the paired bootstrap is byte-deterministic across two runs and its control cases hold (identical systems p = 1, a 5 % perturbation seen), the dry run lists exactly the nine files with their hashes and refuses every stray file, and nothing touches the network.

Non-goals:
- No neural scorer. COMET-22 and MetricX-24 are `null` with a recorded reason unless per-segment scores are handed in through the seam (T8); B1 and B2 of the master plan stay open (their weights cannot be fetched here and their unverified internals need a checkpoint to be verified).
- No real data and no real model: every number of the gate is the toy's and ranks nothing. No FLORES+, NTREX or human-set row is read by D13's tests; the real FLORES+ fetch, the real baselines and the real Hub are never contacted. No GPU (the machine has one the owner did not authorise): every Eole, converter and CTranslate2 process of a test or measurement runs with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=""` on `cpu`; `--device cuda` exists, is validated and was never run. No real upload: `--upload` exists and is never run.
- No mechanism and no change to what a system does (D12's rule): D13 measures. No tokenizer is built (B3 and B4 stay open: F2). No benchmark decontamination of training data, no document-level evaluation of a document model, no zero-shot evaluation, no repeated-look accounting for devtest beyond the structural guard of T2.
- Nothing is fine-tuned or continued from a pretrained model: the external models (NLLB, MADLAD, Hy-MT2) are evaluation baselines only, as the project's rules say; no Inner Mongolian data or variety (FLORES+ `khk_Mong` and any language code outside the registry are refused by name), no Latin-script informal Mongolian, no tuning on FLORES+ devtest. Nothing in D13 touches the topics the project's content rules in CLAUDE.md exclude: the card, the release record, the workflow, the configs, the docs and the tests carry no such field and no such sentence (T16, T22).

## 2. Findings that bind the design (spike)

1. sacrebleu 2.6.0's per-segment sufficient statistics exist (private methods `_extract_corpus_statistics`, `_compute_score_from_stats`), are integers (10 per segment for BLEU, 18 for chrF, 24 for chrF++) and a corpus score equals the score of their sums EXACTLY; the statistics are not mutated; the signature appears once a metric has seen text; corpus chrF is not the mean of sentence chrF (96.99 against 98.01); an empty direction raises `IndexError` (F1).
2. Offline BLEU tokenizers: `13a`, `zh`, `char`, `intl`, `none`; `ja-mecab` and `ko-mecab` need MeCab, which is not installed; `flores200`, `flores101`/`spm` and `spBLEU-1K` DOWNLOAD a model on first use (the spike's first run did, F17); B3 and B4 were never built (F2).
3. sacrebleu's paired bootstrap gives p = 0.000999 (its minimum) for a system against ITSELF, reads its seed from the environment (`SACREBLEU_SEED`, default 12345: p 0.082 against 0.097 for 12345 against 7) and works in float32; it is not used (F3).
4. A counter-based splitmix64 index stream (reproducing the published vectors) with integer sums gives an exact, deterministic bootstrap: numpy and pure Python agree index for index and sum for sum; 1,000 resamples take 0.02 to 0.09 s for 1,000 to 3,832 segments; draw counts are uniform; the seed moves an interval end by about 0.02, so it is fixed and recorded (F4). Document resampling widens the interval by 1.5 to 2.2 (F5). A mean-type metric is exact through integer micro-units (F6).
5. `datasets` costs 0.68 s to import (lazy only); a load without a cache under the offline switches raises `ConnectionError` without a socket; patching `datasets.load_dataset` runs the real `acquire.hf.fetch` offline, and `fetch` has no `revision` parameter and no test (F7). The real FLORES+ layout is not known offline (F7).
6. `huggingface_hub` builds `CommitOperationAdd` objects from local files without a network call; it accepts some bad repository paths (`/abs`, `a//b`, empty), so the allowlist is ours; the real client raises `OfflineModeIsEnabled` before a socket; the library's card template carries a field our rules exclude, so the card is rendered by our own function (F8). The export's `manifest.json` holds absolute local paths and is never uploaded (F9).
7. PyYAML reads the key `on` of a workflow as the boolean `True`; `ci.yml` pins five actions by tag; a draft `release.yml` parses and passes the static checks (F10).
8. A report rendered from real results is byte-identical across `PYTHONHASHSEED`, locale and time zone (F11). One engine call translates 3,832 toy rows in 2.2 s (F12).
9. The engine answers an untrained pair with status `ok` in the wrong language (`zero_shot=True`) and raises `ConfigError` for the whole batch when one target is outside the model (F13). D6's `CT2Translator` is silent about a family whose language tokens are not in the vocabulary (F14).
10. The toy gate can pin: chrF++ 97.03 / 99.05 / 98.97 / 98.38 for the float32 export and 36.50 / 46.09 / 29.30 / 31.67 for the D11 teacher on the four plain directions (480 rows each); paired deltas of +52.97 to +69.67 with p = 0.0020; a 2 % perturbation gives delta +1.2 and p = 0.0020; float32 against int8 gives p between 0.108 and 0.448 with intervals that touch zero (F15). The golden suite's literals come from the pure-Python reference (F16).

## 3. Rulings

T1. Scope and the amendments to the master contract. D13 builds what the master plan's D13 line names, plus the seams it needs, and nothing that changes what a system does. Three amendments to `<contracts>` "Eval result", each recorded in section 7 when built: **C2** `created` is not in `result.json` (the bytes of a result are a function of its inputs, as D12's T13; `created` lives in the manifest of the run); **C3** `manifest_sha` keeps its name and means the SHA-256 of the manifest that vouches for the system's model (the export's `manifest.json`), `null` for a baseline or a hypotheses file (the manifest of the RUN cannot be in a file it lists), and the file sits one level deeper, `<out>/<suite>/<system>/<direction>/result.json` (the master plan's `eval/<suite>/<system>/result.json` names one `direction` per file and the suite has up to 16); **C4** the result gains `chrf_pp_signature` (chrF++ has its own signature, `nw:2`), `statuses`, `unmeasured` (a reason for every null metric), `robustness_coverage` and `robustness_source` (D12's review: a block without its coverage hides the denominators), `bootstrap` (what the intervals are) and `provenance` (T11). D12's C1 stands: `robustness` has the six keys.

T2. Suites: kinds, splits and the guards. A suite is a set of sentences in several languages that systems are asked to translate and are scored against. `kind` is `benchmark` (FLORES+ or NTREX, read from files fetched or provided), `human` (an export of D2's annotation app) or `fixture` (generated by a test or by a pipeline's own fixtures: never evidence of translation quality); `split` is `dev`, `devtest` or `test`; a benchmark is `dev` or `devtest`, a human set `test`, a fixture any. The suite's PURPOSE follows from the split: `dev` is for model selection, `devtest` and `test` are for reporting. **The structural guard (never tune on devtest):** (a) `Suite.selection_view(direction)` raises `ConfigError` unless the split is `dev`, and is the only function that hands rows to a selection loop; (b) `rinchen eval report` refuses (exit 3) a run whose suite is a `dev` suite, so a number computed on dev can never be reported as a result; (c) an AST scan (`tests/unit/test_eval_devtest_guard.py`) shows that no module under `src/rinchen` outside `rinchen.eval` and `rinchen.cli.eval*` imports `rinchen.eval.suites`, `rinchen.eval.suite_rules` or `rinchen.eval.suite_sources` (training, synthetic-data, distillation and serving code cannot read a benchmark), and the scan is itself tested on a string that violates it; (d) a run's manifest and `run.json` record the purpose, so the report names it. **`khk_Mong` is refused by name** (and every code outside the language registry with it) wherever a language code is read: the suite reader, the builders (a language directory, a file name, a column of an export), the fetch configuration; the message names the rule and the code and says nothing else. A suite of `benchmark` `flores_plus` additionally refuses `mn_Mong` (FLORES+ has no traditional-script Mongolian that the project uses; traditional-script output is judged on human sets and by D12's suites). **The 16 directions:** the language registry has nine non-`mn_Mong` languages; Khalkha (`khk_Cyrl`) is the hub, the other eight (`supported_partner_languages()`) are its partners, and `standard_directions()` lists `khk_Cyrl-p` and `p-khk_Cyrl` for each, sorted, 16 in all. FLORES+ is multi-way parallel (the same ids in every language): a direction takes the rows that have both languages and uses the other side's text as the reference, so the same 1,012 (devtest) rows serve both ways of a pair; the sentences of FLORES were translated from English, so a direction whose source is not English reads a translation (translationese), recorded in the runbook as a known limit. A direction exists in a suite only when at least one row has both languages; a language a source lacks is recorded (`source.missing_languages`) and the directions it would have made are absent from the suite's `directions`, shown by the report as `n/a (not in the suite)` against the 16 the targets expect; nothing is dropped silently. A human set is DIRECTIONAL: its directions are the `(src_lang, tgt_lang)` pairs of its rows and the reverse of a pair is never added (the source of a reverse direction would be a translation).

T3. The suite file. Schema `rinchen.eval.suite/1`, UTF-8 JSON, `ensure_ascii` off, sorted keys, two-space indent, one final LF: `{schema, name, kind, benchmark, split, source: {dataset, revision, pinned, files, missing_languages}, directions: [...], rows: [{id, doc, text: {language: sentence}}]}`. `name` and `benchmark` match D12's id pattern; `source.dataset` is a string or null, `revision` a string (a commit hash) or null, `pinned` true only with a revision, `files` maps the relative name of every input file the builder read to its SHA-256 (the suite is bound to its inputs), `missing_languages` a sorted list; `directions` are `<src>-<tgt>` registry codes, unique, source different from target, each with at least one row that has both; a row has a unique `id` (`[A-Za-z0-9._:-]{1,96}`), `doc` (null or an id: the document the sentence belongs to) and `text` with at least one language, each sentence non-blank, at most 5,000 characters, no line break, no lone surrogate and no reserved symbol of the project that a model reads as a control piece other than a numbered placeholder (`source_symbols()`: the engine would refuse the whole batch). The reader (`Suite.from_bytes`) is as strict as D12's: exactly these fields, each of its type, a repeated key refused (D12's `parse_object` hook), the `kind`/`split` pairs of T2, every language a registry code (`khk_Mong` by name), `DataError` naming the place (row index and id, field) and never quoting a text, at most 100,000 rows and 64 MiB (the cap is checked before the parse). `read_suite(path)` reads the bytes once and returns the suite and the SHA-256 of the bytes it parsed; the commands hash with `hash_input` and parse the hashed bytes (`verified_content`). `Suite.view(direction)` gives the sources, references, ids and, when every row of the direction has a `doc`, the documents, in file order (the draws of the bootstrap are bound to positions, so the order of the rows is part of the suite).

T4. Sources, the loader seam and the network. Three readers turn LOCAL files into a suite, all in `rinchen.eval.suite_sources` over plain rows, so no test needs a dataset or a network: **parquet shards** (FLORES+ as `rinchen eval suite fetch` writes it: one directory per language holding `shard-*.parquet`; the columns named by the configuration, `id` and `text` and optionally `doc`, are read; a missing column is a `DataError` naming it; the rows of the languages are joined by `id` and every language must have exactly the ids of the hub language, in the order of the hub file: a language with more or fewer ids is refused with the counts, never intersected), **lines directory** (NTREX or any multi-way parallel text: `<dir>/<registry code>.txt`, one sentence per line, the line number is the id (`l000001`), a blank line is refused with its number, all files of one length) and **human export** (D2's `annotate export`: JSON Lines, TSV or Parquet with the columns `src, tgt, src_lang, tgt_lang, doc_id, seg_idx, set, origin, split`; every row `origin` `human` and `split` `test` and one `set`, else refused; the id is `<doc_id>-<seg_idx:05d>` or, without a document, `s<ordinal:06d>` in the order of the sorted rows; duplicates refused). The Parquet reading is done by the command module with polars (`glob=False`), never by `rinchen.eval`. **The fetch** is the only step that touches the network: `rinchen eval suite fetch` calls `rinchen.acquire.hf.fetch(dataset, dest, config=<language>, split=<split>, revision=<pinned commit>)` for each language of the configuration; D13 adds the `revision` keyword to `fetch` (default `None`, behaviour unchanged, passed to `datasets.load_dataset`; the one edit of D1 code) and refuses an unpinned revision unless the operator says `--unpinned`, in which case the suite records `pinned: false` and the report says so. Tests run the REAL `fetch` with `datasets.load_dataset` patched by a recording fake (F7); no test of D13 touches a socket (a fixture makes every `socket.connect` raise). `configs/eval/suites.yaml` (validated by `rinchen.config.eval_suites`) holds, per benchmark, the reader, the Hub dataset id and the column names (the FLORES+ id `openlanguagedata/flores_plus` and the columns `id`, `text`, `url` are recalled from memory and UNVERIFIED offline: the configuration says so in a comment and the builder never assumes them), the splits, `revision: null` (the operator pins it before a real fetch) and the languages to fetch (the registry minus `mn_Mong`).

T5. Systems and what they can run. A system is anything that turns the requests of a suite into outputs: `rinchen` (an export directory, through the engine), `baseline` (a generator of D6's config: `ct2` NLLB or MADLAD, `vllm` for Hy-MT2, `llamacpp`) or `hypotheses` (a file, the boundary for every other system). `rinchen.eval.systems` defines the adapters over protocols (no engine, synth or HTTP import): `EngineSystem` wraps anything with `translate(rows, options)` and `info` (`Engine` fits), `CandidateSystem` wraps D6's `Translator` protocol (`translate(texts, src_lang, tgt_lang) -> list[list[str]]`, the first candidate is the output, an empty list is `no_output` with an empty text), `FileSystem` holds the outputs read from a file. **Which directions a system runs (F13):** an engine system runs a direction only when it is in `info.directions` (the pairs the model was trained for); any other direction of the suite is listed as `not_trained` with the model's trained pairs named in the run summary, and is never translated or scored (a zero-shot pair would measure the wrong language); a baseline runs a direction when both languages are in its family's code table (all nine languages are in NLLB's and MADLAD's), else `unsupported_language`; a file system runs the directions the file holds (a file may cover some directions, each complete: every id once, no other) and lists the rest as `not_in_file`. Every direction of the suite (or of the `--direction` selection) appears in the run summary with its state: `run`, `not_trained`, `unsupported_language`, `not_in_file` or `not_selected`; none is dropped. **Baseline decode settings:** the synthetic-data defaults (`n` 4, `temperature` 0.7) are sampling settings; the command overrides them (`n` 1, `temperature` 0.0, `top_p` 1.0; for CT2 the beam stays as configured, `n` 1) and records the effective settings in `system.options`; `device` `auto` is refused and `cuda` is validated and never run here. **The vocabulary guard (F14):** before a CT2 baseline translates, the command reads `shared_vocabulary.json` of its model directory and refuses (exit 2) a family whose language tokens (NLLB: the codes of both languages; MADLAD: the `<2xx>` target tag) are not in it; the toy export is the negative control. The guard, the files and the configuration are hashed first like every input. The GPU is never touched by D13; a baseline on `cuda` is an operator's decision, unmeasured.

T6. The runner. `run_suite(suite, system, directions) -> SuiteRun` makes ONE call to the system with every request of the runnable directions in a fixed order (directions in the suite's order, rows in file order); a system that must group by direction does so inside its adapter and returns one output per request in order; a count mismatch is an `UpstreamError` naming the counts. An `Output` is a `text` and a `status` (the engine's five statuses; a baseline gives `ok` or `no_output`); a status that is not `ok` is not an error: the text (possibly empty or partial) is scored as it is and the statuses are counted per direction in the result, so a failure the system flagged stays visible beside the metric (D12's rule). The outputs file is `hypotheses.jsonl`: JSON Lines, one object per request with the keys `direction`, `id`, `status`, `text`, sorted keys, `ensure_ascii` off, the order of the requests, at most 128 MiB; reading takes the SHA-256 of the bytes once and parses those bytes, and refuses an unknown direction or id, a repeated request, an incomplete direction, a bad status or a text with a lone surrogate (`DataError` naming the direction and id when they are well formed, never a text). EVERY run writes it, holding the outputs of the directions that ran as canonical bytes (a model or baseline run writes what the system wrote; a file run writes the canonical rewrite of the part of the file it used), so a run directory is self-contained and the report re-scores from it; a file system reads one; so any system, including a rerun after a metric change, enters through the same file.

T7. Metrics. `bleu`, `chrf` and `chrf_pp` (chrF++, `word_order` 2) by sacrebleu with its defaults, at CORPUS level only, computed from per-segment sufficient statistics (`rinchen.metrics.segment_stats`; F1): the statistics of every segment are extracted once and every score, including each bootstrap resample's, is sacrebleu's `_compute_score_from_stats` of a sum of integers, clamped to 100.0 as `corpus_scores` clamps. A sentence score is never averaged into a corpus score (D12's M-2). The module is pinned by tests that compare it with `rinchen.metrics.bleu_chrf.corpus_scores` with `==` on the golden corpus, on the toy sentences and on empties, so a change of sacrebleu's private API or numbers fails loudly; `bleu_chrf.py` is not changed. The BLEU tokenizer follows the TARGET language: `zh` for `zho_Hans`, `char` for `jpn_Jpan` and `kor_Hang`, `13a` for everything else; the allowed set is `13a`, `zh`, `char`, `intl`, `none`, and a metric built with any other tokenizer (`flores200`, `flores101`, `spm`, `spBLEU-1K`, `ja-mecab`, `ko-mecab`) is a `ConfigError` before sacrebleu is asked (F2). The signature of each metric, as sacrebleu reports it after the statistics are extracted, is recorded per direction (`bleu_signature` names the tokenizer, `chrf_signature`, `chrf_pp_signature`): `nrefs:1|case:mixed|eff:no|tok:13a|smooth:exp|version:2.6.0` for a Latin or Cyrillic target. chrF and chrF++ are the cross-language figures; chrF++ is the release metric (the README's "chrF++ in at least 12 of 16 directions"); for a target written without spaces (`zho_Hans`, `jpn_Jpan`) the word part of chrF++ is degenerate, which the runbook says. A direction with no segment is a `DataError` (sacrebleu raises `IndexError`); an empty hypothesis and an empty reference score as sacrebleu scores them.

T8. COMET-22, MetricX-24 and MetricX-24-QE: the seam, no scorer. D13 builds NO neural scorer: the checkpoints cannot be fetched here, the master plan's own text calls the key mapping, the score index (250089) and the EOS handling UNVERIFIED until a checkpoint and Eole's native scorer exist, the models need a GPU for practical throughput (XLM-R large; MetricX XL), and a scorer that nothing could verify would be a stub presented as a feature. The three metrics are `null` in every result with a reason in `unmeasured` (`no segment scores were given: no scorer is built (B1 and B2 are open)`). The SEAM: `rinchen eval run --hypotheses FILE --segment-scores comet22=FILE` (repeatable; only `comet22`, `metricx24`, `metricx24_qe`; only with `--hypotheses`) attaches per-segment scores produced elsewhere (Eole's native scorer, a later B1 or B2): a JSON Lines file whose first line is the header `{"schema": "rinchen.eval.scores/1", "metric": ..., "suite_sha256": ..., "hypotheses_sha256": ...}` followed by `{"direction", "id", "score"}` lines; the header must name the suite and the hypotheses file of THIS run (a score of other hypotheses is refused: provenance), every segment of every direction the run scored must have exactly one finite score (`|score| <= 1000`), and a metric given for some directions only is refused. The corpus value is the MEAN of the segment scores (the system-level COMET and MetricX figure); its interval is the bootstrap of the mean through integer micro-units (`round(score * 1e6)`, `[sum, count]` per unit: F6), so the same machinery serves; MetricX scores are lower-is-better (`HIGHER_IS_BETTER` says so, and a comparison reports it). The seam is tested with files that fake scorers write (the tiny random-init parity models D15 mentions would plug into it, to exercise the code path). What the seam does not do: say whether a score is right.

T9. The bootstrap. Module `rinchen.eval.bootstrap`; a result's `bootstrap` object records `{resamples, seed, alpha, interval, rng, unit, units}`. **Rule:** for a direction with `n` units, the index of position `j` of resample `b` is `((mix(seed + (b * n + j + 1) * 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15) >> 32) * n) >> 32`, `mix` splitmix64's finaliser and every operation modulo 2^64 (name `splitmix64-counter/1`; F4: it reproduces the published splitmix64 vectors; numpy's generator is NOT used, so no numpy version can change a draw); the total statistic of a resample is the sum of the integer rows of the drawn units; a score is `score_from_statistics` of that sum; the interval is `[s[k], s[B - 1 - k]]` of the sorted scores with `k = floor(B * alpha / 2)` (no interpolation: positions 25 and 974 for the defaults), a percentile interval. Defaults `resamples` 1,000, `alpha` 0.05, `seed` 20,261,110 (a literal of `rinchen.eval.bootstrap` and of `configs/eval/metrics.yaml`; the seed is FIXED and recorded: the seed moves an end by about 0.02 (F4)); `resamples` below 100, `alpha` outside (0, 0.5) or a negative seed is a `ConfigError`. **The unit** is the document when every row of the direction has a `doc`, else the segment (a document bootstrap keeps the sentences of an article together; F5); the units are numbered in the order their document first appears in the rows; the result records which and how many; with fewer than 2 units the interval is `null` (a one-unit "interval" is a point). All metrics of one direction use the same draws. Everything between the integer rows and sacrebleu's score formula is integer arithmetic; on another machine the only exposure is the C library's `exp` and `log` inside BLEU (chrF has none), which is why the golden tests compare floats at 1e-9 and the integer sums exactly. Cost: 1,000 resamples take 0.02 to 0.09 s per metric and direction at 1,000 to 4,000 segments (F4); a 16-direction report is seconds.

T10. The paired comparison. For two systems scored on the SAME rows of one direction, `compare(a, b)` draws ONE set of indices and uses it for both (a paired bootstrap): `delta` is `score(a) - score(b)` on the full rows, `delta_ci` the percentile interval of the resample differences (T9's rule), `p_value = min(1, 2 * min((le + 1) / (B + 1), (ge + 1) / (B + 1)))` with `le` the resamples whose difference is at most 0 and `ge` those at least 0 (two-sided, add-one smoothing, so identical systems give exactly 1.0 and the smallest p is `2/(B + 1)` = 0.001998), and `better_fraction`, the share of resamples in which `a` is better (above for higher-is-better metrics, below for lower-is-better). **The verdict** of a comparison is its p-value against `alpha`: `a` is significantly better iff `delta` is better than zero and `p_value < alpha`; the interval is shown but never decides (F15: ends that touch zero while p is 0.108 because of ties). There is NO correction for multiple comparisons: the report says so in a sentence that states the number of intervals and comparisons it holds. `sacrebleu.significance` is never called (F3); the hidden seed of the environment cannot reach a result. Identical systems give delta 0, interval [0, 0] and p 1.0; swapping `a` and `b` negates `delta` and the interval and leaves p alone; a constant offset on every segment gives a zero-width interval (the draws are shared).

T11. The result. `result.json` (schema `rinchen.eval/1`, amended by T1) per (suite, system, direction): `{schema, system, suite, direction, n_segments, trained, statuses: {status: n}, metrics: {bleu, chrf, chrf_pp, comet22, metricx24, metricx24_qe}, bleu_signature, chrf_signature, chrf_pp_signature, unmeasured: {metric: reason}, ci95: {metric: [lo, hi] | null}, bootstrap, robustness: {six keys} | null, robustness_coverage | null, robustness_source | null, manifest_sha, provenance: {suite_sha256, outputs_sha256 (the digest of the run's own `hypotheses.jsonl`), system: {kind, name, model_sha256, manifest_sha256, options, outputs_sha256 (the digest of the file a file system was read from, else null)}}}`. Sorted keys, two-space indent, LF, no timestamp, floats as Python writes them, rounded nowhere; a metric with no value is `null` and its reason is in `unmeasured` (never 0). `trained` is true for a baseline or a file system and, for an engine system, whether the direction is in `info.directions` (always true for a direction that ran). `robustness` is the block of the system's D12 run (T12), the SAME in every direction of the system (the six values are not per direction); `robustness_source` names the suite and the digests it came from so that nobody reads it as measured on this benchmark. `run.json` (schema `rinchen.eval.run/1`) is the run's table of contents: the suite and its purpose, the system record, the settings, one entry per direction of the suite with its state and reason, and for each file written its relative path and SHA-256 (`files`); the report reads a run through it. A strict reader (`result_from_bytes`, `run_from_bytes`) refuses unknown keys, bad types and a metric listed in neither `metrics` with a value nor `unmeasured`.

T12. The robustness input (what D12 moves and keeps). D12's command and files stay as they are (`robustness.json`, the narrow `report.json` of schema `rinchen.report.robustness/1`, the manifest of stage `eval_robustness`; `eval/robust_report.py` is kept as the per-system artefact: its `report.json` is exactly the carrier of the block and its `coverage`). D13 consumes them through `rinchen.eval.robustness_input`: `--robustness DIR` (the output directory of `rinchen eval robustness`) hashes `robustness.json` and the narrow `report.json` BEFORE reading them, parses the hashed bytes, refuses a directory whose manifest is not of stage `eval_robustness` or does not list both digests, a narrow report whose `robustness_sha256` is not the digest of `robustness.json`, and a `robustness` block in the report that differs from the one in `robustness.json`; it embeds `robustness` (the block, unchanged) and `robustness_coverage` (unchanged: never the block alone) and records `robustness_source` `{suite, suite_sha256, robustness_sha256, report_sha256, binding}`. **The binding** to the system: for an engine system the robustness run's `model_sha256` and `manifest_sha256` must equal the engine's (binding `model_digest`), else exit 2 (the block would describe another model); for any other system the robustness run's system `name` must equal the run's (binding `name`, recorded as the weaker one). D13's report is `rinchen.report/1` (T14), a different file from D12's, so nothing of D12 is renamed or removed (the master plan's D3 to D12 must-not-change rule).

T13. The targets. `configs/eval/metrics.yaml` lists the README's release targets as data: `beats_baseline` (chrF++ above the named baseline system in at least 12 of 16 directions of the FLORES+ devtest, by T10's verdict), `off_target` (at most 1 %: UNMEASURED always, D13 runs no language identification and says so), `numbers` (D12's `numbers_acc` at least 0.99) and `placeholders` (`placeholder_acc` equal 1.0). `evaluate_targets(config, comparisons, results, robustness)` returns for each `{id, description, threshold, value, status, basis}` with `status` one of `met`, `not_met`, `unmeasured` and a `basis` sentence: `met` needs the count or the value over the whole expected set (all 16 directions measured), `not_met` when the threshold can no longer be reached (wins plus the directions still unmeasured fall short, or the value is below), else `unmeasured` with the reason (for example `4 of 16 directions measured`). The evaluation is a measurement: no command exits non-zero because a target is not met, and the report and the card print the table whatever it says.

T14. The report. `rinchen eval report` reads run directories and suites and writes `report.json` (schema `rinchen.report/1`) and `report.md`. `report.json`: `{schema, evidence: {kinds, fixture_only}, systems: [...], suites: [...], runs: [{suite, system, run_sha256}], coverage: [{suite, system, direction, state, reason}], results: [the result of every (suite, system, direction), without its robustness fields], robustness: {system: {block, coverage, source}}, comparisons: [{suite, direction, system, baseline, metric, higher_is_better, delta, ci95, p_value, better_fraction, significant, unit, units}], targets, bootstrap, note_multiple_comparisons}`, sorted keys, LF, no timestamp and NO local path (a run is named by its suite, system and the digest of its `run.json`; a scan test finds no absolute path in a generated report). `evidence.fixture_only` is true when every suite is of kind `fixture`. The comparisons are made against `--baseline SYSTEM` for every other system of the same suite and direction (chrF++ and BLEU; the statistics are recomputed from the verified suite and outputs, so a result file is never trusted to hold more than it says). `report.md` is rendered by one pure function of the parsed report: sections in a fixed order (title and evidence banner; systems; suites; one table per suite and metric with a row per direction in `standard_directions()` order then the suite's others and a column per system, each cell `value [lo, hi]` with two decimals or `n/a (<state>)`; the paired comparisons with delta, interval, p and a mark where significant; robustness; the unmeasured list; the targets; provenance with the first 12 hex digits of each digest), `format(x, ".2f")` for scores and `.3f` for p, dictionaries with sorted keys, `\n` line ends, no timestamp: byte-identical across processes (F11) and pinned by a golden file. A report that includes a run of a `dev` suite is refused (T2). When a baseline is named that has no result for a suite and direction, the comparison is absent and the coverage table says why.

T15. The commands. Exit codes as D12's: 2 a bad option, an export not vouched for, a refused device, a model or file that changed after it was hashed, a missing or unusable `HF_TOKEN` for `--upload`, a vocabulary that lacks the family's tokens; 3 an unreadable or invalid input (suite, outputs, scores, robustness, run, report, stage), a foreign manifest in the output directory, a `dev` run in a report, a stage that is not the one its release record vouches for, `khk_Mong`; 4 an upstream failure (CTranslate2, an HTTP generator, the Hub); 1 the output cannot be written; 130 interrupted. Every command hashes every input BEFORE it reads it and parses the bytes it hashed (`hash_input`, `verified_content`), makes `verify_export` vouch for a model, refuses an output directory that is an input's and a foreign manifest (`begin_commit`), writes its files and the manifest LAST, and prints no text of a suite or an output in any message. (a) `rinchen eval suite fetch --benchmark B --split S --out-dir D [--revision HEX | --unpinned] [--config FILE]` (stage `eval_suite_fetch`; the only network step: never run here), `rinchen eval suite build --benchmark B --split S --from PATH --out-dir D [--name N] [--kind benchmark|human] [--config FILE]` (stage `eval_suite`: the readers of T4, the suite file `<name>.json`, inputs hashed one by one, the fetch manifest of the shards cross-checked when present) and `rinchen eval suite inspect --suite FILE` (prints the name, kind, split, purpose, row counts per language, directions and SHA-256; exit 3 for an invalid suite). (b) `rinchen eval run --suite FILE --out-dir D (--model DIR | --generator NAME [--generators-config FILE] | --hypotheses FILE) [--system NAME] [--direction DIR ...] [--beam-size 4] [--max-decoding-length 256] [--script-mask retry|always|off] [--device cpu|cuda] [--threads 1] [--robustness DIR] [--segment-scores METRIC=FILE ...] [--resamples N] [--seed N] [--metrics-config FILE]` writes `<D>/<suite>/<system>/{hypotheses.jsonl, <direction>/result.json, run.json, manifest.json}` (stage `eval_run`; exactly one of the three system options; the options of D12's `RunOptions` for `--model`; the old files of this (suite, system) are removed first by the list of the old manifest; prints `suite:`, `system:`, `purpose:`, one line per direction (`eng_Latn-khk_Cyrl: chrF++ 97.03 [95.87, 98.05] (480 segments, 480 ok)` or `khk_Cyrl-deu_Latn: n/a (not_trained)`), `run:` and `manifest:`; exit 0 whenever it measured). (c) `rinchen eval report --run DIR ... --suite FILE ... --out-dir D [--baseline SYSTEM] [--metrics-config FILE]` (stage `eval_report`: each run is read through its `run.json` whose digest the run's manifest lists, then every file `run.json` names with its digest; each suite file is matched to its runs by SHA-256, not by path; refuses a run whose suite is not among the given files). (d) `rinchen release stage` and `rinchen release hub` (T16 to T18). `rinchen eval robustness` is unchanged.

T16. The model card. `rinchen.export.model_card.render_card(record, evidence, files, repo_id) -> str` is a pure function of the export's record (`rinchen.json`), the parsed report (`rinchen.eval.report_read`), the facts of the staged files (name, bytes, SHA-256) and the repository id: UTF-8 Markdown with a YAML front matter, no timestamp, `\n` line ends, byte-identical for the same inputs (golden file). The front matter holds only `language` (the registry's ISO 639-3 prefixes of the languages of the model; `mn_Mong` is a script of `khk`), `library_name: ctranslate2`, `pipeline_tag: translation` and `tags` (`rinchen`, `translation`, `ctranslate2`, `mongolian`, plus `toy` when the evidence is fixture-only); no `model-index` (results are in the body, labelled), no `datasets`, no `base_model` (the model is trained from random initialisation, and the card says so), and no key outside this list; whether the Hub accepts these values is UNVERIFIED offline. **The honesty rules:** the card opens with a banner that is a function of `evidence` (`fixture_only`: "These figures come from generated fixture suites. They test the tooling and say nothing about translation quality."; mixed or measured: the kinds and the number of directions measured); every table names the suite, its split, its kind, the number of segments, the bootstrap (1,000 resamples, seed, percentile 95 %), the unit and the sacrebleu signature of BLEU and chrF++; every value that was not measured is the word `n/a` with its reason (`not_trained`, `not in the suite`, `no segment scores were given`), never a blank, 0 or 1; the robustness section prints the six values with their coverage (`scored` of `total`) and says they come from the named robustness suite; a sentence of the card claims nothing a table does not show (no "state of the art", no ranking without the p-value, no statement about directions that were not measured); the limits section is a list of facts (which directions are trained, that zero-shot pairs are not evaluated, that devtest was reported and dev used for selection only, that comparisons are uncorrected, the units, what is unmeasured: COMET, MetricX, off-target, GPU, real-world input). Sections: title, banner, what it is (architecture sizes and quantization from the record, "trained from random initialisation" stated as the project's rule with the checkpoint's manifest stage beside it (the record cannot prove it), the tag placement and the target tags, the script `Cyrl` default), directions, files (name, bytes, SHA-256), usage (`rinchen.Translator(path).translate(text, tgt="khk_Cyrl")`; no URL is invented), evaluation, robustness, release targets, limits, provenance (the checkpoint's kind, step and digests, the export manifest's digest, the report's digest, `rinchen_version`). **The Hub's own card template is never used and the card has no field, heading or sentence about the topics the project's content rules in CLAUDE.md exclude** (F8): that is a consequence of those rules recorded here, and a test scans the generated card and every D13 source, document and config for the excluded vocabulary (the needles are built from code points in the test, so the test file does not hold them).

T18.
