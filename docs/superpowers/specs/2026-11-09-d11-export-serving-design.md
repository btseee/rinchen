Status: binding for the D11 plan (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-09-d11-export-serving.md`), written before the build; the last task of the plan amends it to the finished code (section 7, amendments, as D10's section 8). The runbook is `docs/runbooks/11_serving.md`. Section 7 was filled by Task 12 on 2026-11-10 (code tip `4bfaa5e`); sections 1 to 6 stay as they were written, and where the build differs section 7 says how, why and on what evidence. Master plan: `docs/research/2026-10-29-rinchen-dev-agent-prompt.md`, milestone D11 (and D12, D13, D15 for what D11 must leave room for). Evidence for every fact about Eole, CTranslate2, FastAPI, Docker and the packaging: `.superpowers/sdd/2026-11-09-d11-export-serving/spike-findings.md` (a spike on this machine, Eole 0.6.4, CTranslate2 4.8.4, CPU, toy data; scratch scripts named there). Everything GPU-side is unmeasured and labelled so.

## 1. Goal and non-goals

Goal: a model of ours that anyone can run. A checkpoint (a final step or an averaged directory that our manifest vouches for) becomes an export directory with a record of its own (`rinchen.json`) and a quantisation chosen at conversion; an engine reads such a directory (verified once, by hash), translates with the guards that CTranslate2 does not have (end token, source limit, tag check, reserved symbols, script suppression), and is offered as a Python class (`rinchen.Translator`), a command (`rinchen translate`, stdin to stdout) and a service (`rinchen serve`: FastAPI, a micro-batcher, Prometheus text metrics, a graceful stop that drains the queue) with a CPU container recipe. Parity between Eole and CTranslate2 and the quantisation loss are measured by commands and gates. STOP gate (master plan: "`docker compose up` serves tiny model"): through the real binaries on the CPU a tiny teacher is exported to float32 and int8, the strict parity gate (Eole greedy against CTranslate2 float32 greedy: at least 95 % of 200 sentences equal, mean |delta log-probability per token| at most 1e-3), the quantisation gate (int8 chrF at most 1.0 below float32 on the tiny dev set) and the script gate (0 forbidden-script characters in 500 outputs with suppression on) pass, `rinchen translate` and `rinchen.Translator` agree with the engine, and `rinchen serve` (the command line the compose file holds, run natively) answers 64 concurrent requests with all 200 and p95 under 2 s, exposes its counters on `/metrics` and drains on SIGTERM. The container itself (`docker build`, `docker compose up`) CANNOT be proven here and is recorded as UNMEASURED (T20).

Non-goals:
- No GPU was used and none is claimed (the machine has one that the owner did not authorise). `--device cuda`, `float16`, `int8_float16` on a GPU, token-based batching and every throughput or latency figure at real size are unmeasured and labelled so. No `Dockerfile.gpu` is written: nothing about it can be built or run here (a deliberate departure from the master plan's file list, T20).
- No real model exists: the toy teacher translates templated sentences, so every quality number tests machinery and ranks nothing.
- No placeholder, terminology or markup engine (`infer/placeholders.py`, `terminology.py`: D12 owns the robustness suites and their metrics), no `terminology` field in the request, no sentence-splitting of a sentence-mode text, no language identification beyond the script, no rate limiting by client, no authentication, no model hot reload, no multi-model server, no `Dockerfile.gpu`, no `export/model_card.py` and no `export/hub.py` (D13), no evaluation suites (D12, D13), no `infer/preprocess.py`, `postprocess.py` or `batching.py` (the steps are inline in the engine and the batcher is `serve/batcher.py`).
- Nothing is fine-tuned and nothing continues a pretrained model: an export is of a checkpoint OUR launcher wrote and our manifest vouches for.
- No Inner Mongolian data or variety, no Latin-script informal Mongolian (the project's content rules in CLAUDE.md apply to code, tests, docs and messages as everywhere).

## 2. Findings that bind the design (spike)

1. Strict parity holds by three orders of magnitude for float32: 200 of 200 equal for the teacher, the student and a target-tag teacher; mean |delta log-probability per token| 8.3e-7 (max 3.9e-6) against the gate 1e-3. Eole's `pred_score` equals CTranslate2's `score_batch` sum only with `length_penalty: none`; `score_batch` appends `</s>`; for the `tgt` placement Eole's score excludes the forced prefix token and `score_batch` includes it (drop `log_probs[0]`: the error of a sentence total falls from 0.48 to 3.9e-6, about 0.05 to 4e-7 per token).
2. The log-probability bound is a float32 bound: int8 1.45e-3 mean (max 2.8e-2), int8_float16 1.69e-3, int16 2.4e-4; chrF within 0.07 of float32 for all (int8 +0.06: the toy saturates at 98.2).
3. All eight quantisations convert (2.2 to 2.6 s); on this CPU only float32, int16, int8 and int8_float32 run; a stored GPU type loads as float32 or int8_float32 with a warning on stderr, an explicit unsupported `compute_type` is a `ValueError`; `compute_type="auto"` quantises EVERY export (float32 included) to int8_float32. The int8 file is 73 % of float32 on the toy (29 % on a 6/6 d512 random-init model); int8 was 10 times faster than float32 for one sentence on one thread (mid-size model, noisy machine).
4. `suppress_sequences` takes single-token entries that must be entries of `shared_vocabulary.json` (`ValueError` otherwise), never `</s>`; it does not touch a forced prefix; the cost is the per-call list conversion (0.55 microseconds per entry), not a per-step cost. 500 outputs of requests the toy was never trained for held a forbidden-script letter in 96 outputs without suppression and in 0 with it; the D5 converter wrote traditional script in 448 of 448 outputs without and 0 with.
5. The vocabulary does not say which languages a model was trained for (every vocabulary holds all 118 reserved symbols since D9), but the saved `config.json` of a checkpoint our launcher wrote does: its `data` corpora are named `<src>-<tgt>.<group>`.
6. CTranslate2 `translate_batch` is thread-safe and releases the GIL: `asynchronous=True` and several Python threads give the same throughput; `inter_threads` raises throughput about linearly (toy), `intra_threads` lowers a call's latency and, above 1, hurts one-sentence calls on the toy.
7. Everything needed is locked: FastAPI 0.162.0, Starlette 1.10.0, uvicorn 0.54.0, httpx 0.28.1, prometheus-client 0.26.0, pydantic 2.14.1, pydantic-settings 2.16.2, structlog 26.1.0. `ASGITransport` does not run the lifespan (`app.router.lifespan_context(app)` does); `TestClient` as a context manager does; no `pytest-asyncio`. 64 concurrent requests: all 200, p95 48 ms (real uvicorn, localhost), 24 to 28 ms (in process); SIGTERM with 24 in flight: all 24 answered, exit status -15.
8. The wheel lacks `configs/languages.yaml` (an installed package cannot load the registry); a frozen `uv sync` installs torch and 17 CUDA wheels that serving never imports (the CLI imports with torch, eole and transformers blocked).
9. A document model exports and decodes through CTranslate2; the end-token check makes the D9 chunker see a cut window (39 of 64 at `max_decoding_length` 20) and fall back.
10. `docker compose config` validates a compose file offline (exit 0); no Dockerfile linter is installed; nothing was built or run.

## 3. Rulings

T1. The export directory and `rinchen.json`. An export is a directory of `model.bin`, `shared_vocabulary.json`, `config.json` (the converter's), `spm.model` (a copy of the SentencePiece model the checkpoint was trained with), `rinchen.json` (the record) and, last, `manifest.json` (stage `export`, status `export: ok`, the five files by name with their SHA-256). `rinchen.json` is `rinchen.model/1`: UTF-8 JSON, sorted keys, two-space indent, one final LF, `ensure_ascii` off, no timestamp (the same inputs give the same bytes). Fields, all required, no others: `schema`; `name` (`[A-Za-z0-9._-]{1,64}`, default `rinchen-<first 12 hex of the SHA-256 of model.bin>`); `rinchen_version` (the installed package's version at export); `tag_placement` (`src` or `tgt`); `directions` (sorted `<src>-<tgt>` registry codes: the directions the model was trained for); `languages` (the sorted union of their codes); `tags` (a map from each TARGET code of `directions` to its direction tag, such as `<2khk_Cyrl>`); `document_level` (bool: the model decodes `<doc>` windows); `quantization` (what the converter was asked for, one of `QUANTIZATIONS`, T5); `checkpoint` (`kind`, `step`, `weights_sha256`, `config_sha256`, `vocab_sha256`, `manifest_sha256`, `manifest_stage`: D10's `CheckpointRecord` unchanged); `spm_sha256`; `encoder_layers`, `decoder_layers`, `hidden_size`, `vocabulary_entries` (read from the checkpoint). `from_bytes` is as strict as D10's (this schema, every field and no other, each of its type, positive counts, 64-hex digests, kind and step agreeing, a repeated key refused) and also refuses `languages` that is not the union of `directions`, `tags` whose keys are not the targets of `directions`, a direction that is not two distinct registry codes, `directions` not sorted and unique. `verify_export` also refuses (`ConfigError`) a `tags` entry that is no longer the registry's tag of that language (the registry changed after the export): the export is stale, re-export. The provenance chain: the checkpoint's `train_launch` or `train_average` manifest (which, for a D9 continuation or a D10 student, names its own sources) -> `rinchen.json.checkpoint` -> the export manifest -> the engine (`Engine.info` keeps the SHA-256 of `model.bin` and of the manifest) -> `/readyz`, the start log line and every parity or evaluation manifest that names the export.
T2. Where the record and its reader live, and what moves out of `train` (the layering rules are kept: `train` never imports `script_conv` or `cli`; `infer` imports nothing of `train`, `cli`, `serve`, `synth`, `script_conv`; `export` imports nothing of `train`, `synth`, `cli`, `script_conv`; `serve` imports nothing of `train`, `cli`, `synth`, `script_conv`, `annotate`). Into `rinchen.export`: `record.py` (`ModelRecord`, `CheckpointRecord`, the constants `EXPORT_STAGE`, `RECORD_FILE`, `SPM_FILE`, `CT2_FILES`, `EXPORT_FILES`, the literal `ContinuationKind`, which `train.checkpoints` then imports), `verified.py` (`VerifiedExport`, `verify_export`, `verified_content`, `json_object`, and from `train/distill_export_files.py` `verified_bytes`, `shared_vocabulary`, `end_token`), `quantize.py`, `ct2.py` (`convert`, T4). Into `rinchen.infer`: `source_tokens.py` (`ct2_source_tokens` and the reserved-piece rule of `train/distill_teacher.py`, imported back there so that the two translators share one copy of the rules that must change together). Stays in `train`: `distill_export.py::export_checkpoint` (it needs `continuation.verify_source` and the saved-config readers: it verifies, gathers the facts and calls `convert`), `distill_saved_config.py` (which gains `saved_directions` and `saved_document_level`), `distill_teacher.py` and the rest of D10. `train/distill_export_files.py` is deleted. The edits to D10 are the imports of eight source files and about seventeen test files (listed in the plan) and the tests of the moved code, which move with it.
T3. Migration from D10. `rinchen.json` REPLACES `export.json` and stage `export` replaces `distill_export`; there is no second reader. A directory that holds `export.json` and no `rinchen.json` is refused by `verify_export` with a message that names the remedy (re-export the checkpoint: 3 s for the toy; no real export exists); `rinchen distill export` is the same function as `rinchen export convert` registered under both names (its options and output lines are D10's plus `--quantization`, `--direction`, `--document-level`, `--name`), so D10's runbook commands keep working. The D10 teacher translator, `distill generate` (its fingerprint takes the digests of the same files, so a float32 export gives the same fingerprint inputs as before) and the D10 gate use the new reader; the D10 gate's numbers do not move.
T4. `convert` (`export/ct2.py`). `convert(checkpoint, out_dir, *, facts, spm_bytes, inputs, name=None, timeout=None) -> ConvertResult(record, manifest_path)`: `facts` are the checkpoint-derived fields of the record (placement, directions, document level, quantization, the `CheckpointRecord`, the counts); `spm_bytes` are the bytes whose SHA-256 the caller verified; `inputs` the manifest's inputs with the digests the caller took (hash once: `convert` never hashes an input again). It makes a private directory `.<name>.<random>` beside `out_dir` (D10's staging, kept: the converter refuses an existing directory), runs `export_ct2(..., quantization=facts.quantization)` into a child with the GPU hidden, requires the converter's three files, cross-checks `vocabulary_entries` with `shared_vocabulary.json`, writes `spm.model` and `rinchen.json` (through `replace_file`), hashes the five files once, writes `manifest.json` last (paths named after the FINAL `out_dir`), renames the child onto `out_dir` after a last existence check, and removes the private directory on any failure (and `out_dir` only if this call put it there); the converter's log stays beside the output as `<out_dir>.export.log`. `export_ct2` gains one keyword `quantization: str | None = None` that appends `--quantization <value>` (nothing is appended for None: the converter keeps the stored type). The command writes nothing of its own beyond printing.
T5. Quantisation. `QUANTIZATIONS = ("float32", "float16", "int8", "int8_float16")`: `float32` the parity reference and the default, `int8` the CPU product, `int8_float16` and `float16` the GPU products (converted here, never run on a GPU: unmeasured). The converter accepts four more (`int8_float32`, `int8_bfloat16`, `int16`, `bfloat16`); they are not offered (one tuple entry each if a later need appears) and the spike's numbers for them are in the findings. Quantisation is applied AT CONVERSION and recorded in `rinchen.json`; at load the engine passes `compute_type="default"` (what the file stores; the device falls back with CTranslate2's own warning, which the engine logs once with the effective type) unless the operator names another; `"auto"` is refused (`ConfigError`: it quantises a float32 export on a CPU), `device="auto"` is refused as in D10. `quantize.py` holds `QUANTIZATIONS`, `require_quantization`, and `effective_compute_type(quantization, device)` (cpu: float32 -> float32, int8 -> int8_float32, int8_float16 -> int8_float32, float16 -> float32, measured; cuda: the stored type, unmeasured). The int8 gate: the same checkpoint converted at float32 and int8, translated greedily by the engine over the tiny dev rows, chrF of int8 at least float32's minus 1.0 (measured +0.06); the mean |delta log-probability per token| against Eole is REPORTED for a quantised export, never gated (T12).
T6. The engine. `rinchen.infer.engine.Engine` is the loaded model: thread-safe, batch-level, used by the service, the command, the parity and the gates. `rinchen.infer.translator.Translator` (exposed as `rinchen.Translator`, T15) is the convenience facade over one engine. Types (frozen slotted dataclasses; errors are `ConfigError`, `DataError`, `UpstreamError` as everywhere): `LoadOptions(device: "cpu" | "cuda" = "cpu", compute_type: str = "default", inter_threads: int = 1, intra_threads: int = 1, max_queued_batches: int = -1)`; `DecodeOptions(beam_size: int = 4, n: int = 1, max_decoding_length: int = 256, max_source_pieces: int = 256, max_batch_size: int = 32, script_mask: "retry" | "always" | "off" = "retry")` (D10's validation: strict integers of at least 1, `n <= beam_size`, `max_source_pieces <= 1023`, D10's `MAX_SOURCE_PIECES`); `Row(text: str, src: str | None, tgt: str, script: "Cyrl" | "Mong" | None = None)`; `Translation(text, status, alternatives, src, tgt, script_purity, retried, zero_shot)` with `status` in `ok`, `empty`, `too_long`, `truncated`, `no_output`. `Engine.open(directory, load=LoadOptions(), *, script_converter=None)` verifies the directory (`verify_export`, hash once), reads `shared_vocabulary.json`, `config.json` (`eos_token`, no default) and `spm.model` through the verified bytes, checks EVERY tag of `rinchen.json.tags` (an entry of the vocabulary and one SentencePiece piece: a model that cannot be asked for its own languages is refused at load, not at the first request) and loads `ctranslate2.Translator` (imported lazily). The constructor takes seams for tests (`translator`, `processor`) as D10's does. `Engine.translate(rows, options) -> list[Translation]` in input order, one result per row, never fewer, never silently dropped. `Engine.translate_documents(documents, src, tgt, options, *, script=None) -> list[DocumentResult]` (T10). `Engine.info` (name, quantization, device, effective compute type, placement, directions, targets, document level, `model_sha256`, `manifest_sha256`), `Engine.counters()` (a copy), `Engine.close()`. The pipeline per row, in this order: (1) resolve the target (T8); (2) refuse a lone surrogate (`DataError` naming the index, as D10); (3) normalise with `text.normalize.normalize(text, src or hint)` (`hint` is `mn_Mong` when the text holds a letter of the Mongolian script, else `eng_Latn`: `normalize` depends on the language only for `mn_Mong`); (4) a text with no letter or digit is `empty` and not sent; (5) encode with the model's SentencePiece processor; no piece -> `empty`; more than `max_source_pieces` -> `too_long`; neither is sent (CTranslate2 would cut a source of 1,024 pieces silently); (6) build the source by placement (`ct2_source_tokens`); (7) group the sent rows by the suppression list they use (T9) and make ONE `translate_batch` per group with `beam_size`, `num_hypotheses=n`, `max_decoding_length`, `max_batch_size`, `return_end_token=True`, `disable_unk=True`, `suppress_sequences`, and `target_prefix` for `tgt`; (8) per hypothesis, in this order: strip the forced prefix once (a hypothesis that does not start with it is an `UpstreamError` naming the row index, never the text), note a missing end token (`truncated`), drop (and count `reserved_symbol`) a hypothesis holding a piece no translation holds (the unknown piece, the end token inside, a reserved symbol the model is not meant to write, any vocabulary entry that is no SentencePiece piece), decode, turn a line-break character into a space (counted `line_break`: the command's one-line contract), strip; (9) the first surviving hypothesis is `text`, the others are `alternatives` (distinct, in order); a row all of whose hypotheses were dropped is `no_output` with the empty text, a row whose best hypothesis was cut is `truncated` and KEEPS the partial text (the service and the command say so; D10's KD drops it, a user is better served by a flagged partial); (10) script purity and the retry (T9); (11) the script conversion of a `Mong` request for a model with no `mn_Mong` target (T8). Counters (a dict, cumulative since `open`, updated under one lock by the whole call only: a failed call adds nothing): `sources`, `empty`, `too_long`, `truncated`, `no_output`, `reserved_symbol`, `line_break`, `zero_shot`, `script_retries`, `script_retry_improved`, `script_impure`, `script_conversions`, `converter_rule_fallbacks`, `converter_invalid_outputs`, `windows`, `window_fallbacks`.
T7. Shared guard rules. D10's rules about CTranslate2 silences are the engine's too and live once: `infer/source_tokens.py` holds `ct2_source_tokens(placement, tag, pieces)` (`src`: `([tag, *pieces], None)`, `tgt`: `(pieces, [tag])`) and `reserved_pieces(processor, end_token, vocabulary, *, allow=())` (the unknown piece, the end token, every reserved symbol of the project except those in `allow` (a document request allows `<sep>`; the placeholders `⟦n⟧` are always allowed: D12's engine will write them), every vocabulary entry that is no SentencePiece piece). `train/distill_teacher.py` imports both (its counters, its options and its own file reads are unchanged).
T8. Directions, languages and the script option. The engine accepts a target that is a key of `rinchen.json.tags`; any other registry code is a `ConfigError` naming the model's targets. `src` is a hint, not an input of the model (the tag names only the target): `None` means "unknown" (normalisation by the script hint, no sentence splitting); a given `src` must be a registry code. A pair that is not in `directions` is allowed and marked `zero_shot` (counted; the model was not trained for it, nothing more is claimed); `src == tgt` with a given `src` is a `ConfigError`. `script` applies to a Mongolian target only (`khk_Cyrl` or `mn_Mong`; any other target with a script is a `ConfigError`, exit 2 or HTTP 400): `Mong` selects the target `mn_Mong`, `Cyrl` the target `khk_Cyrl`, `None` keeps the code asked for. When the selected target is not a target of the model: if a script converter is attached (an object with `convert(text, target) -> Conversion`, D5's `ScriptConverter` fits; `infer` takes it as a Protocol and imports nothing of `script_conv`) and the request is `Mong`, the engine translates into `khk_Cyrl` and converts the final text (counters `script_conversions`, `converter_rule_fallbacks`, `converter_invalid_outputs`; the converter is word-level and its own limits stand, D5); otherwise `ConfigError`. A `Cyrl` request for a model that lacks `khk_Cyrl` is a `ConfigError`. A source in traditional script needs nothing: the tag is the target's and the SentencePiece model reads the text; `normalize` keeps NNBSP and the Mongolian format characters for `mn_Mong`.
T9. Script masking. `rinchen.infer.script_mask`: `forbidden_scripts(tgt) -> frozenset[Script]` (every member of `rinchen.lang.scripts.Script` that the registry does not give to `tgt`; so for `khk_Cyrl` the Mongolian script, Latin, Han, Hangul, Hiragana and Katakana; for `mn_Mong` Cyrillic and the rest), `script_hint(text) -> "mn_Mong" | "und"` (`mn_Mong` when the text holds a letter of the Mongolian script), `script_purity(text, scripts) -> float` (the share of the letters of `text`, counted as `lang.scripts.script_of` does, that are in `scripts`; 1.0 for a text with no counted letter, unlike D6's private `_purity`, which gives 0.0 and is left alone), `suppression_list(vocabulary, processor, tgt, *, end_token, allow=()) -> list[list[str]]` (the entries of `shared_vocabulary.json` that are: no SentencePiece piece, or the unknown or a control piece, except the end token; a reserved symbol the model must not write (T7); a normal piece holding a letter of a forbidden script (the piece with U+2581 read as a space); a byte-fallback piece `<0xNN>` of an ASCII letter when Latin is forbidden; byte pieces of other bytes stay: a byte piece alone says no script), built once per (target, `allow`) and cached, never holding `</s>`. Modes: `always` decodes every row with its list; `retry` (the default, the master plan's post-check) decodes without a script list, and a row whose output has letters and `script_purity` below 0.98 (`PURITY_FLOOR`, a prior) over the scripts of its target is decoded again with the list, counted (`script_retries`, and `script_retry_improved` when the second purity is higher; the second output replaces the first only then); `off` never suppresses a script (the control entries, such as `<blank>` and the padding tokens, are always suppressed: they are never text). Purity is reported on every `Translation`; a final output below the floor counts `script_impure`. The cost is a list conversion per CTranslate2 call (0.55 microseconds per entry, 30,000 entries 16 ms: measured as a per-call figure, extrapolated to a real vocabulary). Whether a hard mask helps a real model (legitimate Latin names in Mongolian output are impossible under `always`) is unmeasured: `retry` is the default for that reason and `always` is the gate's mode.
T10. Document mode. `Engine.translate_documents` splits each document with `split.rules.split(document, src)` (`src` is required: a `ConfigError` when `None`), and runs `doc_chunker.translate_document` with callables that go through the engine: a window is the source `[tag, "<doc>", *pieces of the joined window]` and is asked for the end token, and a window that lacks it returns `""` (which makes the chunker fall back to the sentence callable for that window as a whole: counted `window_fallbacks`); the sentence callable is the ordinary row path. It needs `rinchen.json.document_level` true and placement `src` (D9 built windows for `src` only), else `ConfigError`. The result is `DocumentResult(text, translations, stats: ChunkStats)` with the translations joined by one space (no space for `zho_Hans` and `jpn_Jpan` targets). A window is translated by one CTranslate2 call (sequential per window: throughput of a document request is unmeasured). `document_level` is recorded at export: derived from the saved config when it has a `data` section (some corpus's `src_prefix` holds the word `<doc>`), stated by `--document-level` otherwise, and a statement that contradicts a derivable value is refused (as the placement is).
T11. Concurrency. `Engine` holds one `ctranslate2.Translator` with `inter_threads` replicas; `translate` is called from any number of threads (the library releases the GIL; SentencePiece encode and decode are thread-safe); the engine's only mutable state is the counters (one lock) and the suppression-list cache (built under a lock, immutable once built). The service runs `Engine.translate` on a `ThreadPoolExecutor(inter_threads)` so that up to `inter_threads` batches run at once, never on the event loop. `inter_threads x intra_threads` must not exceed the cores of the process; the defaults are `1 x 1` for the library and the command and `2 x 1` for the service. The measured scaling (toy and mid-size random-init model, noisy) is in the runbook with its labels.
T12. Strict parity (`rinchen.train.export_parity`, command `rinchen export parity`). The library sits in `train` because it starts Eole (`eole_run` helpers) and reads a checkpoint; it imports the reader from `export` and the engine seams from `infer`. `strict_parity(checkpoint, export, sources, *, out_dir, threads, timeout) -> ParityReport` where `sources` are `(text, tgt_lang)` rows: tokenise with the export's SentencePiece model and `ct2_source_tokens`; run `eole predict` once (greedy, `transforms: []`, `max_length: 128`, `max_length_ratio: 0`, `with_score: true`, `length_penalty: none`, `tgt_file_prefix` with the tags for `tgt`; `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=""`, `gpu_ranks: []`); run CTranslate2 greedy with the export's own compute type; `exact` = equal token lists (Eole's tokens against CTranslate2's without the end token and, for `tgt`, without the prefix); for the log-probabilities score Eole's tokens with `score_batch` (drop `log_probs[0]` for `tgt`), delta per token = |sum(log_probs) - pred_score| / len(log_probs). `ParityReport`: `sentences`, `exact`, `exact_share`, `mean_abs_delta`, `max_abs_delta`, `quantization`, `placement`, the first ten mismatches (token lines, never more), and `gated` (true only for `float32`). `passes` = `exact_share >= 0.95` and, when `gated`, `mean_abs_delta <= 1e-3`; a quantised export reports both numbers and `passes` needs the exact share only. The thresholds are the master plan's and are never lowered. The checkpoint must be the one the export record names (its weights SHA-256 equals `record.checkpoint.weights_sha256`, else `ConfigError`: a parity of two different models is meaningless).
T13. `rinchen translate` (top-level command). `rinchen translate --model DIR --tgt CODE [--src CODE] [--script Cyrl|Mong] [--beam-size 4] [--n-best 1] [--max-decoding-length 256] [--max-source-pieces 256] [--batch-size 32] [--script-mask retry|always|off] [--device cpu|cuda] [--compute-type default] [--inter-threads 1] [--intra-threads 1] [--doc] [--format text|jsonl] [--input FILE] [--output FILE] [--strict] [--script-converter-ct2 DIR --script-converter-spm FILE]`; `--model` also reads `RINCHEN_MODEL`. Input is UTF-8 read strictly (invalid UTF-8 is exit 3), one text per line (a trailing CR is dropped; lines are split at LF only), translated in chunks of `--batch-size` lines as they are read, output flushed per chunk: exactly one output line per input line, in order (an empty or untranslatable line is an empty line). With `--doc` the input is blocks separated by blank lines, each block one document, output one translated document per block (its sentences joined by spaces on ONE line), blocks separated by a blank line. `--format jsonl` writes one object per line (`text`, `status`, `alternatives`, `src`, `tgt`, `script_purity`). A summary goes to stderr (`translated N lines: ok A, empty B, too_long C, truncated D, no_output E`, plus the engine's script counters) and names the first line numbers of each problem; the exit code is 0 unless `--strict` and some line was not `ok` (then 4, after the output is written). Other exit codes: 2 a bad option, a model our manifest does not vouch for, an unknown or unsupported language or script, a refused compute type, a D10 directory; 3 unreadable or invalid input or a lone surrogate; 4 CTranslate2 failed; 1 the output cannot be written; 130 interrupted. The model is loaded before any input is read; nothing is written to stdout when the model is refused. No GPU is used unless `--device cuda` is given (unmeasured).
T14. The Python API and the package. `rinchen.Translator(directory, *, device="cpu", compute_type="default", inter_threads=1, intra_threads=1, script_converter=None)`; `.translate(texts: str | Sequence[str], *, tgt, src=None, script=None, beam_size=4, n=1, max_decoding_length=256, max_source_pieces=256, script_mask="retry") -> Translation | list[Translation]` (a str gives one result, a sequence a list), `.translate_document(document: str | Sequence[str], *, tgt, src, ...) -> DocumentResult`, `.info`, `.counters()`, `.close()` and the context-manager protocol. `rinchen/__init__.py` exports `Translator` lazily (PEP 562 `__getattr__` and `__dir__`, a `TYPE_CHECKING` import, `__all__`): `import rinchen` stays at about 0.06 s and loads neither `ctranslate2` nor `sentencepiece` (a test checks `sys.modules` in a subprocess). `[project.scripts]` is unchanged (`rinchen = rinchen.cli.main:app`; `python -m rinchen` exists). The language registry becomes package data: `pyproject.toml` gets `[tool.hatch.build.targets.wheel.force-include] "configs/languages.yaml" = "rinchen/_data/languages.yaml"` and `config/languages.py` resolves the registry as the repository copy when it exists and the packaged copy otherwise (`LANGUAGES_PATH` keeps naming the repository copy; `load_languages(path=None)` resolves at call time). A serving image installs without the torch family (T20).
T15. The service. `rinchen.serve.app.create_app(settings, engine=None) -> FastAPI` (`engine` is the seam of the tests; without it the lifespan opens the export). Endpoints: `POST /v1/translate`, `GET /healthz` (200 while the process runs), `GET /readyz` (200 with `{"status": "ready", "model", "quantization", "device", "compute_type"}` once the engine is loaded and a warm-up translation ran and until the drain begins; else 503), `GET /metrics` (Prometheus text, T17). Request (`extra="forbid"`; the master plan's contract without `terminology`, which D12 adds): `texts: list[str]` (1 to `max_texts`, at most 64; each at most `max_text_chars`, default 5,000), `src: str = "auto"` (a registry code or `auto`: `auto` is the script hint, `mn_Mong` or `und`, and reported as `detected_src`), `tgt: str`, `script: "Cyrl" | "Mong" | null = null`, `beam_size: int = 4` (1 to 8), `n_best: int = 1` (1 to `beam_size`), `doc_mode: bool = false`. Response (the master plan's four fields plus): `translations: list[str]`, `detected_src: list[str]`, `model: str`, `latency_ms: float`, `statuses: list[str]` (one of the engine's statuses per text), `alternatives: list[list[str]]` (present only when `n_best > 1`), `script_purity: list[float | null]`. A text that is `empty`, `too_long` or `truncated` is a 200 with its status, never a failure of the request. Errors always `{"error": {"code": str, "message": str}}` (the validation handler is replaced so that 422 has this shape too): 400 `bad_request` for a `ConfigError` of the engine (unknown language, target not served, script on a non-Mongolian target, `doc_mode` on a sentence model), 422 `validation_error` (schema), 413 `payload_too_large` (the body is over `max_body_bytes`, default 1 MiB, by `Content-Length` and by counting the bytes of a chunked body), 503 `draining` or `overloaded` with `Retry-After: 1` (T16), 504 `timeout` (`request_timeout_seconds`, default 30), 500 `internal` for anything else (the message is generic, the cause is logged; no text of a request is ever logged or echoed). The service binds `127.0.0.1` by default; there is no authentication and the runbook says to put a gateway in front.
T16. The micro-batcher and the stop (`serve/batcher.py`). `MicroBatcher(run, *, max_batch_sentences, max_wait_seconds, max_queue_sentences, workers)`: `await submit(key, items) -> list[result]` queues the items of one request atomically (all or none), per `key` (the decode options that CTranslate2 takes once per call: beam, n, `max_decoding_length`, `max_source_pieces`, the script mode); a batch of one key is dispatched when it holds `max_batch_sentences` or when its oldest item has waited `max_wait_seconds`, to `workers` threads (at most `workers` batches in flight: under load batches grow by themselves); `run(key, items)` is the engine call. `QueueFull` when `queued + len(items) > max_queue_sentences` (HTTP 503 `overloaded`), `Draining` after `close()` began (503 `draining`). `close(timeout)` stops accepting, dispatches what is queued, waits for everything in flight, and returns the number of items it had to fail with `Draining` after `timeout` (never a silent loss: those requests get the 503). An exception of `run` fails every item of that batch with it. The lifespan on shutdown flips readiness to false first, then closes the batcher, then the engine. uvicorn does the rest: on SIGTERM it stops accepting connections, waits `timeout_graceful_shutdown` (the setting `shutdown_timeout_seconds`, default 20) for the requests in flight, then runs the lifespan; the process then ends by the re-raised signal (exit status -15, 143 in a shell, measured), which the runbook and the compose file (`stop_grace_period`) account for. A document request is a batch item of its own key and runs `translate_documents` in a worker.
T17. Metrics and logs. ## 6. Open questions, recorded as such

- Everything GPU-side: CTranslate2 on CUDA, float16 and int8_float16, token batching, the speed of a 1.1 B teacher or a 190 M server student, the memory of a replica, the real value of `inter_threads` and `intra_threads`, the cost of `retry` and of a 30,000-entry suppression list at real size.
- The real quantisation loss (the toy saturates at 98.2: int8 -0.06), the real strict-parity margin on a real model in float32 (it is 1e-6 on the toy), whether a quantised real model keeps the greedy output of float32.
- Whether a hard script mask helps or hurts a real model (the toy's leaks come from requests it was never trained for; real leaks and legitimate Latin names in Cyrillic output are unmeasured); the purity floor 0.98 is a prior; the policy for languages other than the two Mongolian scripts is the registry's, uncalibrated.
- Docker: the image build and size, `docker compose up`, the base and uv image tags, the health check and the stop behaviour inside a container (PID 1), the model volume layout of a real deployment, a GPU image.
- Document mode: throughput (a call per window), joiners beyond a space, `tgt` placement (D9 built `src` windows only), a document model of any real quality.
- `rinchen translate` startup pays 1.0 to 1.2 s of imports (`rinchen.cli.main` loads every command); a lighter entry point or lazy command loading is not built. A serving image that skips more than the CUDA family (eole, transformers, the data tools) needs an extras split of `pyproject.toml`, which D11 does not make.
- Directions are derived from the saved config's corpus names; a checkpoint that was not written by our launcher needs `--direction`. Zero-shot pairs are allowed and flagged, never judged.

**A14. Readiness and the drain are not observable over HTTP (T16, T17; Task 9, review I4; `38c490b`).** T15 and T16 described a readiness that turns false when the stop begins and 503 `not_ready` and `draining` answers. Under `uvicorn.run` those states are never on the wire: the start of the lifespan runs BEFORE the socket is bound and the end of it AFTER the listener is closed and the connections drained (uvicorn `Server.startup` and `Server.shutdown`, `D-UVICORN-LIFESPAN-ORDER`). **A15. The stop has one budget (T16, T18, T19, T20; review I1; `a291106`).** T16 said uvicorn waits `timeout_graceful_shutdown` for the requests in flight and then runs the lifespan, which closes the batcher with the same timeout. The two waits added up: a stuck batch made a stop take twice `shutdown_timeout_seconds` (a fake engine stuck 8 s with a 2 s timeout ended the process 4.18 s after the signal; with the default 20 s and the compose `stop_grace_period: 30s` that is SIGKILL (137) and no `shutdown_drained`). Not done, on purpose: giving the batcher the REMAINING budget (it needs a `uvicorn.Server` subclass that records when the signal arrived and would lose `uvicorn.run` and its exit status). Consequences: the requests in flight get `ceil(T/2)` (10 s with T=20), and an app driven in process waits `drain_wait_seconds` at the end of its lifespan (a timeout under 1 s used to cancel them at once; since the re-review it is refused, A25). The compose file's `stop_grace_period` must be at least `shutdown_timeout_seconds + 5 s` (a test pins it; 30 s against T=20 holds). The exit status is the signal's (-15, 143 in a shell), not 0 (`D-SERVE-SIGTERM-EXIT`). The engine is closed last, and only when no batch is still running in it (`engine_left_loaded` is logged otherwise; a timed-out batch keeps its worker thread until it returns).
